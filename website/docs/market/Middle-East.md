# Middle East

Saudi Arabia runs the clearest example of a state platform for electronic promissory notes; Bahrain and Abu Dhabi Global Market enacted the MLETR early but no bill-of-exchange platform has been reported on their basis; Türkiye has drafted but, as far as we have found, not enacted an electronic cheque and note law.

## Saudi Arabia

[Nafith (نافذ)](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/node/4965) — Platform approved by the Ministry of Justice for issuing and managing electronic promissory notes and other enforcement instruments. The creditor requests the note, the debtor approves it after signing in through the national digital identity, and on default the note passes directly to the enforcement courts; the Saudi Central Bank encourages banks to connect to it. Legal basis: the Commercial Papers Law (Royal Decree No. 37 of 1383H) and the new Enforcement Law of 2026 ([Al Tamimi](https://tamimi.com/law-update-articles/the-nafith-platform-and-digital-promissory-notes-in-ksa); [UN DESA good-practice compendium](https://publicadministration.desa.un.org/good-practices-for-digital-government/compendium/nafith-digital-platform)). The platform reported 700,000 notes issued in the first quarter of 2022 ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/621652/SAUDI-ARABIA/Nafith-platform-issues-700K-electronic-promissory-notes-in-Q1-2022)).

**Compared with our bills:** Nafith is built for enforcement: a note between an identified creditor and debtor, linked to the state's identity system and its courts. Our bill is built for circulation: an accepted obligation that passes between bearers without identification at each step. Nafith shows that demand for electronic notes is strong where the instrument is tied to enforcement; our model relies instead on the acceptor's standing, as a bank's acceptance does.

## United Arab Emirates

[ADGM Electronic Transactions Regulations 2021](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/technology/adgm-adopts-electronic-transactions-framework-1.1174780) — Abu Dhabi Global Market, a common-law financial free zone, adopted an electronic-transactions framework aligned with the MLETR; it took part in the 2021 TradeTrust pilot with Singapore (see [Singapore](./Singapore.md)). No bill-of-exchange platform has been reported.

## Bahrain

[Bahrain enacts the MLETR (2019)](https://www.bahrainedb.com/latest-news/bahrain-creates-history-as-first-nation-to-enact-uncitral-model-law-on-electronic-transferable-records) — The first country to enact the Model Law. No electronic bill-of-exchange platform has been reported.

**Compared with our bills (ADGM, Bahrain):** both are jurisdictions whose law would admit an electronic bill of exchange; neither has a platform. They show that a permissive law alone creates no instrument: what is missing is a register and an acceptor, which is what our contract supplies.

## Türkiye

[Ministry of Trade: draft regulation on electronic cheques and promissory notes (2019)](https://ticaret.gov.tr/haberler/bakan-pekcan-cek-ve-bonoyu-elektronik-ortama-tasiyacak-duzenleme-hazir) — A system for issuing, indorsing, guaranteeing and presenting cheques and notes electronically through the banking infrastructure was announced as ready in February 2019; its enactment has not been confirmed. QR-coded paper cheques are in use.

**Compared with our bills:** a bank-routed design under domestic law; our bills circulate outside the banking infrastructure, with a bank in the role of acceptor only.
