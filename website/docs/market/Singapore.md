# Singapore

Singapore was among the first countries to adopt the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Transferable Records and is the home of TradeTrust, the most developed open-source framework for electronic transferable documents. Its bills of exchange law, like ours, descends from the UK [Bills of Exchange Act 1882](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/45-46/61).

## Law 

[Electronic Transactions Act 2010](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/ETA2010) — Amended in 2021 to provide for electronic transferable records in line with the MLETR, covering negotiable instruments, documents of title and bills of lading ([Ministry of Digital Development and Information](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/eta-amended-to-facilitate-transactions-provide-convenience-boost-trade/); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/17725/electronic-transactions-amendment-bill-passed-to-provide-for-electronic-transferable-records-and-electronisation-of-negotiable-instruments-documents-of-title-and-bills-of-lading)).

**Compared with our bills:** Singapore's Act and the UK ETDA pursue the same end by different means: Singapore adopted the MLETR's "reliable method" and "control"; the UK made electronic documents capable of possession. Our bills satisfy either test in the same way, through exclusive control of a token balance. A Singapore-law version of our contract would be legally close to the present one, since the underlying Bills of Exchange Act is the same.

## Systems and projects

[TradeTrust](https://www.imda.gov.sg/how-we-can-help/digital-utilities/tradetrust) — Open-source framework of the Infocomm Media Development Authority for transferable documents, with a token registry of one ERC-721 token per document and the document itself kept off chain ([GitHub](https://github.com/TradeTrust)). **Pilots:** the first digital trade-financing pilot between MLETR-harmonised jurisdictions, with Abu Dhabi Global Market, DBS, Emirates NBD and Standard Chartered, transferring electronic bills of lading (November 2021, [MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2021/worlds-first-digital-trade-financing-pilot-between-mletr-harmonised-jurisdictions)); interoperable electronic bills of lading with India (2023, [IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/press-releases/2023/sg-india-interoperable-electronic-bills-of-lading)). In August and September 2026 its reference application added an obligation lifecycle with accept, reject and discharge actions; no bill-of-exchange product has been documented publicly.

**Compared with our bills:** the nearest active open-source analogue of our acceptance ledger: a government-backed project now records the acceptance of an obligation on chain. TradeTrust individuates each document as a non-fungible token with the document off chain; our bills are fungible units whose terms are on chain. TradeTrust serves trade documents between identified parties; our bills serve payment between bearers.

[DocuTrade pilot on the XDC Network (October 2024)](https://benzinga.com/content/41507288/docutrade-facilitates-breakthrough-in-trade-finance-with-blockchain-based-digital-promissory-notes) — A US$49,518 purchase financed with a digitally signed promissory note handled by a smart contract on the XDC Network, with Propine, a custodian regulated by the Monetary Authority of Singapore.
