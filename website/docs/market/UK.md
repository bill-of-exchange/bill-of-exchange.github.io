# United Kingdom 

The United Kingdom is the jurisdiction whose law our bills are drawn under. Every electronic bill of exchange or promissory note issued in the UK so far has been issued under the same two Acts as ours; the difference lies in what the instrument is and where its register is kept.

## Law 

---
### Legislation 

[UK Bills of Exchange Act 1882](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/45-46/61)

[UK Electronic Communications Act 2000](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/7)

[UK Electronic Trade Documents Act (ETDA) 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/38) — in force since 20 September 2023. Section 2(2) defines an electronic trade document by what a "reliable system" must do: identify the document so that it can be distinguished from copies, protect it against unauthorised alteration, ensure that only one person can exercise control at a time, let that person demonstrate control, and ensure that a transfer removes the transferor's control. Section 2(5) lists factors that may be considered in judging reliability, among them the rules of the system and "the regularity of and extent of any audit of the system by an independent body". Section 3(2) gives an electronic trade document "the same effect as an equivalent paper trade document". The [Explanatory Notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/38/notes/division/2/index.htm) record that before the Act "the law in the United Kingdom did not recognise the possibility of possessing electronic documents".

**Compared with our bills:** the Act is technology-neutral and names no registry. Our answer to the five functions of section 2(2) is the public ledger itself: a token balance exists once and cannot be copied; the contract code cannot be altered after deployment; a balance is controlled by one private key at a time; control is demonstrated by signing a transaction; and a transfer moves the balance from one address to another. The independent audit named in section 2(5) is a factor, not a condition; for a public contract it takes the form of published code verification and of audits that anyone may commission.

## Transactions under the ETDA 2023

[Lloyds Bank and Matalan, 20 September 2023](https://enigio.com/news/lloyds-bank-completes-first-digital-trade-transaction-under-new-uk-legislation/) — The first transaction under the Act, on the day it came into force: a digital promissory note issued by Matalan Retail Ltd to settle a documentary collection for garment purchases, on Enigio trace:original.

[Lloyds Bank and Mercore, 2024](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/45973/lloyds-and-mercore-complete-secondary-trade-transactions-using-digital-documentation) — Digital bills of exchange for sugar shipped from Central America to the UK: the exporter drew the bill, the UK importer accepted it, Lloyds confirmed the acceptance and Mercore funded it; described as the first secondary participations in an accepted digital bill. A four-corner digital documentary collection with a digital negotiable instrument followed in October 2024 ([ITFA](https://itfa.org/landmark-4-corner-dni-transactions-completed/)).

[J.P. Morgan Payments, July 2025](https://www.jpmorgan.com/payments/newsroom/digitizing-trade-finance-eboe) — First electronic bill of exchange under the bank's new product, for MTN South Africa, available at first only for English-law transactions.

[Lloyds Bank, Matalan and Yatex (China), 2025](https://tradetreasurypayments.com/articles/behind-the-scenes-of-the-24-hour-china-uk-import-that-travelled-the-digital-highway) — A digital bill of exchange drafted by Lloyds on Enigio, signed by the Chinese exporter through the TradeGo platform and accepted electronically by Matalan, with all documents exchanged in under 24 hours.


**Compared with our bills:** each of these is a single bill or note for one shipment, drawn on a named party, accepted or confirmed by a bank and then discounted; the instrument is kept as a signed document on a platform (Enigio trace:original in every case where the technology is named). Our bills are issued in any number under one contract with fixed terms, are payable to bearer at sight, and are kept on a public register that the parties do not operate. The platforms, products and funders behind these transactions are described on the [Global](./Global.md) page.
