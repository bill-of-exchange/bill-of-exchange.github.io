Hong Kong, Taiwan and South Korea

Three centralised, bank-mediated systems for electronic negotiable instruments, each built on a national statute rather than on the MLETR. Hong Kong is the only one whose law belongs to the same family as ours.

Hong Kong​

HKMA: e-Cheque — Electronic cheque launched by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the banks on 7 December 2015: a digitally signed file issued and deposited through internet banking, cleared by Hong Kong Interbank Clearing; cross-boundary clearing with Shenzhen since 22 July 2016 (HKMA Insight, 2015). The banks have announced a road map for phasing out paper cheques.

Compared with our bills: the e-Cheque is the only system in the region under a law derived from the UK Bills of Exchange Act 1882 (Bills of Exchange Ordinance, Cap. 19), which makes it the nearest legal comparator to our bills. It is a cheque: drawn on a licensed bank, payable to a named payee, presented once through a bank and not negotiated further in practice. Our bill is accepted by its drawee before it circulates, is payable to bearer, and passes from holder to holder on a public register without a bank in between.

eTradeConnect (2018) — Blockchain trade-finance platform launched by the HKMA with twelve banks for open-account trade; it does not issue negotiable instruments.

Taiwan​

Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan): Annual Report 2003, electronic bills — "Asia's first electronic bill" (電子票據), live since 29 September 2003: a centralised PKI-based registry of the Taiwan Clearing House covering electronic cheques, promissory notes and bills of exchange, with registration, custody, indorsement, presentment and dishonour handled in the system, under the Negotiable Instruments Act (票據法) and the Electronic Signatures Act (電子簽章法).

Compared with our bills: Taiwan showed in 2003 that all three instruments can exist as registry entries with electronic signatures. The registry is a clearing house reached through banks, and the instruments name their payees. Our register has no operator, our bills name no payee, and the signature is the transaction itself.

South Korea​

Act on the Issuance and Distribution of Electronic Promissory Notes (전자어음의 발행 및 유통에 관한 법률) — Statute of 2004, amended several times (most recently Act No. 17354 of 2020), under which electronic promissory notes are issued and transferred through a management institution designated by the Minister of Justice, accessed through the banks. The government has discussed phasing out promissory notes since 2017.

Compared with our bills: a dematerialised Geneva-type note under a special statute: named payee, indorsement in the register, a maturity, mandatory for larger companies. Our bills are bearer sight bills on an open register, voluntary for everyone, and the instrument is a bill of exchange rather than a note.
