China

China operates the world's largest infrastructure for electronic commercial drafts. It is a single state-supervised registry, not a blockchain; the central bank's own blockchain prototype of 2016–2017 was not put into production.

Law and regulators​

People's Bank of China: forum on the reform and development of the bill market — The central bank's account of the first year of the Shanghai Commercial Paper Exchange: electronic bill business expanded and irregular practices curbed. The statutory base is the Negotiable Instruments Law of 1995 (中华人民共和国票据法), supplemented by central-bank rules; the State Council's legislative plan for 2026 does not list a revision of that law (Xinhua).

Systems and projects​

Shanghai Commercial Paper Exchange (上海票据交易所), new-generation bill business system (新一代票据业务系统) — National system for bank-acceptance and commercial-acceptance drafts, operated by the exchange founded in December 2016 under central-bank oversight. The new-generation system, live since June 2022, offers "无成本拆分，无限制流转" (cost-free splitting, unlimited circulation): a draft can be split into sub-drafts that are indorsed or discounted separately. In November 2025 the exchange opened its bill comprehensive service platform to the whole market; in 2025 ICBC alone discounted about 3.3 trillion yuan of manufacturing-sector drafts (Xinhua Finance).

Compared with our bills: the Chinese system and ours arrive at a similar data model from opposite directions. A split draft is a range of serial numbers in which, according to a secondary description, every 0.01 yuan is individually numbered; in our contract every 0.01 shekel is a separate bill. Legally they differ: the Chinese draft stays one bill divided into sub-drafts, while each of our units is a bill in its own right. The Chinese register is central, reachable only through licensed banks with real-name accounts, and holds time drafts of up to six months for trade credit; Chinese law does not allow a draft payable to bearer. Our register is public, our bills are to bearer and at sight, and they are meant for payment.

People's Bank of China: blockchain digital bill prototype (2016–2017) — A blockchain-based platform for bank-acceptance drafts tested by the central bank's digital currency institute with ICBC, Bank of China and WeBank; the test succeeded in early 2017 (Caixin). No production system followed.

Compared with our bills: conceptually the closest institutional project to our thesis that a bank-accepted draft can serve as money, but on a permissioned ledger run by the central bank for interbank trading. Our ledger is public and the drawee's acceptance is recorded in the same contract that the bearer holds.
